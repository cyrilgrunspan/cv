## Preprints

### When is a passage time exactly computable? The noisy saddle-node bifurcation

Cyril Grunspan, 2026. **Submitted.**
[PDF](papers/passage-time-noisy-saddle-node.pdf) (27 pages)

Near a saddle-node bifurcation a noisy one-dimensional system reduces, after rescaling, to a
diffusion with quadratic drift — the normal form of the bifurcation, and in neuroscience the
quadratic integrate-and-fire neuron. A particle entering from the far left lingers where the
equilibrium has vanished and finally escapes. The paper asks when such a passage time can be
computed exactly, and answers at three levels.

For an arbitrary drift, conjugating the generator turns it into a supersymmetric Schrödinger
operator whose superpotential is the drift, so that supersymmetry is a property of the diffusion
rather than an assumption, and the passage time is a sum of independent exponential variables
whose rates are the eigenvalues: computing the law becomes computing a spectrum.

For the normal form that spectrum can be evaluated. The mean is π²(Ai(z)² + Bi(z)²) for every
complex value of the bifurcation parameter z, and factors as 4π²Ai(ωz)Ai(ω⁻¹z); the tail decays
at the Kramers rate, with its prefactor; and an unusually quick passage has probability
−log P(τ ≤ t) ~ A/t³ with A = Γ(1/4)⁸/(192π²) = 15.75606…, a constant independent of the
parameter. Among polynomial drifts the quadratic one is the only one for which this route
closes, so that the universal normal form and the solvable case coincide.

Finally the Laplace transform satisfies an exact cubic functional equation, which places it on
the monodromy surface of the second Painlevé equation and yields the variance in closed form at
the symmetric point.
